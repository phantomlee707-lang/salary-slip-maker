Product Requirements & Development Brief

Product Category: Business & Productivity
Platform: Native Android

Target Audience: Small businesses, micro-enterprises, and independent employers

---

1. Executive Summary

Salary Slip Maker is a lightweight Android application that enables small businesses and independent employers to create professional salary slips without the complexity of a full payroll or human resources management system.

The application allows users to configure their business identity, maintain employee profiles, define earnings and deductions, automatically calculate salary totals, and generate professional PDF payslips for distribution or recordkeeping.

The product is intended for businesses that need formal-looking payroll documents but do not require automated attendance tracking, employee scheduling, statutory payroll calculations, or enterprise HR infrastructure.

The application operates entirely offline. Business information, employee records, salary structures, and generated payslips remain stored locally on the device.

Core value proposition: Professional salary slips, straightforward calculations, and complete control over your business records.

2. Product Objectives

The application must:

- Generate professional, readable salary slips in PDF format.
- Allow users to customize earnings and deduction components.
- Calculate totals automatically as amounts change.
- Support reusable salary templates for recurring payroll.
- Maintain employee and business profiles.
- Preserve previously issued payslips as historical records.
- Allow users to share or print payslips through Android's native sharing interface.
- Support local backup and restoration.
- Work without an internet connection, mandatory account, subscription, or remote backend.

The application must prioritize simplicity, reliability, and document quality over extensive payroll functionality.

3. Target Users

Primary audience

- Small business owners.
- Micro and small enterprises.
- Retail shops and food businesses.
- Home-based businesses with employees.
- Small agencies and independent employers.

Secondary audience

- Freelancers who employ assistants.
- Family businesses formalizing salary documentation.
- Small organizations that prepare salary slips manually.

The application should accommodate users with limited payroll or accounting experience.

4. Functional Requirements

4.1 Business Profile

Users must be able to configure the business information displayed on salary slips.

Supported fields:

- Business or organization name.
- Optional logo.
- Business address.
- Phone number.
- Email address.
- Optional business registration or tax identification number.
- Optional accent color.
- Optional additional business information.

The application must support businesses without logos or formal registration numbers.

Business details should be reusable across payslips. Updating the business profile must not silently alter previously issued documents.

4.2 Employee Management

Users must be able to create, edit, search, and archive employee profiles.

Each profile should support:

- Employee full name.
- Employee ID or payroll identifier.
- Job title or position.
- Optional department.
- Optional contact information.
- Optional notes.

Only necessary information should be collected. Sensitive identifiers and bank details must not be mandatory.

Archiving an employee must preserve their historical payslips.

4.3 Salary Slip Creation

Users must be able to create a salary slip by selecting an employee and entering the relevant payroll period.

Each salary slip should support:

- Unique payslip number.
- Employee details.
- Pay period start and end dates, or a clearly defined payroll period.
- Payment date.
- Currency.
- Earnings breakdown.
- Deductions breakdown.
- Gross earnings.
- Total deductions.
- Net salary payable.
- Optional employer notes.
- Optional payment method or payment reference.

Users must be able to save a draft, edit it, preview it, and issue the final payslip.

The interface should make the distinction between a draft and an issued payslip clear.

4.4 Customizable Earnings

Users must be able to add, edit, reorder, and remove earning components.

Examples include:

- Basic salary.
- Allowances.
- Overtime.
- Bonuses.
- Incentives.
- Commissions.
- Other earnings.

Each earning line should contain a customizable description and monetary amount.

The application must not require a fixed set of earning categories. Users should be able to create business-specific labels.

The gross earnings total must recalculate automatically whenever a line is added, removed, or edited.

4.5 Customizable Deductions

Users must be able to add, edit, reorder, and remove deduction components.

Examples include:

- Tax deductions entered manually.
- Insurance or benefit contributions entered manually.
- Advances or loans.
- Absence deductions.
- Other deductions.

Each deduction line must support a customizable description and amount.

The application must not assume that every business uses the same deductions or that every deduction is legally applicable.

The total deductions must recalculate automatically as the user changes the entries.

4.6 Automatic Salary Calculation

All salary calculations must use a consistent and verifiable formula.

Gross earnings

Sum of all earning line amounts.

Total deductions

Sum of all deduction line amounts.

Net salary

Gross earnings − Total deductions.

Example:

Component| Amount
Basic salary| Rp4,000,000
Transport allowance| Rp300,000
Bonus| Rp200,000
Gross earnings| Rp4,500,000
Tax deduction| Rp100,000
Advance repayment| Rp200,000
Total deductions| Rp300,000
Net salary payable| Rp4,200,000

These figures are illustrative only.

Calculations must update immediately when values change. The application must validate monetary amounts and prevent invalid input from producing misleading totals.

The PDF, preview, and saved record must all display consistent values.

4.7 Salary Templates

Users should be able to save reusable salary templates for employees or employee groups.

A template may contain:

- Default earning components.
- Default deduction components.
- Suggested amounts.
- Default labels and ordering.
- Optional notes.

Templates must make recurring salary-slip creation faster.

Applying a template must copy its configuration into the new draft. Editing a template later must not modify previously issued payslips.

Templates must not silently introduce outdated or incorrect statutory calculations.

4.8 Payslip History

The application must provide a searchable history of salary slips.

Users should be able to:

- View issued payslips.
- Search by employee or payslip number.
- Filter by payroll period.
- View gross earnings, deductions, and net salary.
- Preview or export a previously issued payslip.
- Duplicate an earlier payslip as the basis for a new draft.

Issued payslips must preserve their original financial values, employee details, and displayed business information.

If a correction is necessary, the application should support creating a corrected replacement linked to the original record, with a clear indication of which document supersedes the other.

Issued records must not be silently overwritten when creating the next month's payslip.

4.9 PDF Generation

PDF export is a core feature, not an optional enhancement.

The application must produce professional salary slips suitable for printing, emailing, messaging, and business recordkeeping.

Each PDF should include:

- Business header and optional logo.
- Payslip number and payroll period.
- Employee identity and position.
- Earnings breakdown.
- Gross earnings total.
- Deductions breakdown.
- Total deductions.
- Clearly emphasized net salary payable.
- Payment date, where provided.
- Optional notes.
- Document generation or issuance information as appropriate.

The document should use a clean, restrained, print-friendly visual style.

Layout requirements:

- Consistent typography and spacing.
- Clear section hierarchy.
- Right-aligned monetary values.
- Proper currency formatting.
- Appropriate whitespace between line items.
- No overlapping or clipped text.
- Graceful handling of long employee names and custom descriptions.
- Reliable rendering when optional fields are absent.
- Correct output on standard paper sizes, including A4.

A logo must not be required to produce a polished document.

4.10 PDF Sharing and Printing

Users must be able to:

- Export a payslip to PDF.
- Share the PDF using Android's native Sharesheet.
- Print through compatible Android print services.
- Save exported documents through supported system interfaces.

The application must not upload payslips to an external service automatically.

4.11 Backup and Restore

Users must be able to back up and restore:

- Business profiles.
- Employee profiles.
- Salary templates.
- Draft payslips.
- Issued payslip records.
- Relevant application settings.

Backup files must be handled safely and validated before restoration.

The application should clearly explain whether restoration will replace or merge existing records. Destructive restoration must require explicit user confirmation.

5. User Experience and Navigation

The interface should provide a simple workflow:

1. Configure the business profile.
2. Add employee profiles.
3. Create or select a salary template.
4. Enter the payroll period.
5. Review and adjust earnings and deductions.
6. Preview the salary slip.
7. Save the draft or issue the payslip.
8. Export or share the PDF.

Suggested primary navigation:

- Dashboard: Recent payslips and quick actions.
- Employees: Employee profile management.
- Payslips: Drafts, issued records, and history.
- Templates: Reusable salary structures.
- Settings: Business profile, currency, backup, and preferences.

The application should minimize the number of steps required to create a recurring payslip.

The net salary payable must be visually prominent during editing and preview.

6. Technical Requirements

6.1 Existing Codebase Review

Before implementation, inspect the existing Rainweaver Studio Money Manager project.

Identify its current technology stack, database implementation, navigation patterns, PDF generation approach, build configuration, and reusable UI components.

Reuse suitable, proven components where practical. Do not introduce a new framework or migrate the project without a specific technical justification.

Salary Slip Maker must remain a separate application with independent payroll data and domain logic.

6.2 Local-First Architecture

- All core functionality must operate offline.
- Business, employee, template, and payslip records must be stored locally.
- No mandatory registration or online authentication.
- No backend dependency for calculations, PDF generation, or recordkeeping.
- No unnecessary background services or network requests.

6.3 Data Integrity

- Use consistent monetary arithmetic and rounding rules.
- Recalculate totals from the underlying line items.
- Validate dates, amounts, and required fields.
- Preserve issued payslips as historical records.
- Use safe database migrations.
- Avoid data loss during upgrades.
- Keep preview, PDF, and saved values consistent.

6.4 Security and Privacy

Salary information is sensitive business data.

The application should:

- Keep salary records local by default.
- Avoid logging salary amounts or employee details unnecessarily.
- Avoid transmitting payslip data to third parties.
- Support an optional app lock if feasible.
- Explain where backups and exported PDFs are stored.
- Leave control over sharing and deletion to the user, subject to historical-record safeguards.

An app lock should be described as an additional privacy measure, not as a guarantee of encryption or device-level security.

6.5 Performance and Compatibility

- Keep the APK lightweight.
- Minimize memory usage and startup time.
- Support modest Android devices.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
- Ensure smooth operation with growing employee and payslip histories.
- Test PDF generation on realistic devices and screen sizes.

7. MVP Scope

The initial release must include:

1. Business profile configuration.
2. Employee profile management.
3. Salary-slip creation and editing.
4. Custom earning and deduction lines.
5. Automatic gross, deduction, and net salary calculations.
6. Reusable salary templates.
7. Draft and issued payslip states.
8. Historical payslip records.
9. Professional PDF generation.
10. Android sharing and printing.
11. Local backup and restore.

The MVP must be a dependable document-generation and salary-calculation tool, not a full payroll platform.

8. Out of Scope

The following features are excluded from the initial release:

- Automatic tax calculations.
- Automatic BPJS Kesehatan or BPJS Ketenagakerjaan calculations.
- Attendance tracking.
- Leave management.
- Employee scheduling.
- Automatic salary disbursement or bank transfers.
- Employee self-service portals.
- Cloud payroll synchronization.
- Multi-user approval workflows.
- Full HRIS functionality.
- Accounting ledger integration.

Future statutory payroll features for Indonesia must use clearly documented, versioned, and tested rules. They must not be approximated or presented as authoritative without adequate verification.

9. Acceptance Criteria

The application is ready for initial release when:

1. Users can create and edit business and employee profiles.
2. Users can add arbitrary earning and deduction lines.
3. Gross earnings, total deductions, and net salary calculate correctly.
4. Invalid monetary values are rejected or handled explicitly.
5. Templates can be reused without altering historical records.
6. Draft payslips remain editable.
7. Issued payslips preserve their original data.
8. Corrections can be recorded without silently overwriting the original issued record.
9. Generated PDFs match the saved calculation values.
10. PDFs render correctly with long descriptions and optional fields.
11. PDF spacing, alignment, and pagination are tested.
12. Sharing and printing work through supported Android interfaces.
13. Backup and restore preserve supported records.
14. Core functionality works in airplane mode.
15. Database upgrades do not silently discard existing user data.

10. Development Phases

Phase 1 — Foundation: Inspect the existing project, define domain models, establish local storage, and implement business and employee profiles.

Phase 2 — Salary Calculations: Implement earnings, deductions, automatic totals, draft editing, and reusable templates.

Phase 3 — Payslip Lifecycle: Implement issuance, historical records, duplication, and corrected replacements.

Phase 4 — PDF and Sharing: Build the salary-slip layout, verify calculations and rendering, and integrate native sharing and printing.

Phase 5 — Data Protection and Testing: Implement backup and restore, validate data integrity, test offline operation, and prepare the release build.

11. Product Success Criteria

A small-business owner should be able to prepare a professional salary slip in a few minutes without manually calculating every subtotal or subscribing to a complicated payroll service.

The finished product should make small businesses look more professional while reducing repetitive administrative work.
